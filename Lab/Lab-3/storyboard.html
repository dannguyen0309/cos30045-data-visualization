Exercise 3 / Storyboard

One question.
Three findings.

Audience: Australian TV shoppers who want to compare estimated energy use. The sequence introduces size, checks the technology mix, then supports a more informed comparison.

Wireframes describe layout and reading order; sketch bars are not numerical data.

01 / Context

Size first. Energy next.

Title + audience questionSnapshot + registration count

Explain the 15 Feb snapshot and what one row means before showing a chart.

Reader asks: What does this tell me about choosing a TV?

02 / Size finding

Larger groups, higher average energy

Mean energy: Small / Medium / LargeValues + table + grouping rule

Use three zero-baseline bars, direct values and a table. Explain rounded-size thresholds and kWh/year.

Reader learns: Screen size belongs in the comparison.

03 / Fairness check

Technology groups have different sizes

Mean inches by technologyTechnology labels + sample counts

Show why one overall technology-energy average could mislead.

Reader learns: OLED has a larger size mix in this snapshot.

04 / Within-size comparison

Compare technologies inside size groups

Grouped energy barsShared scale + exact-value table

Use a single energy scale and consistent technology colours. Mention broad bands and small samples.

Reader learns: Technology rankings vary with the size group.

05 / Takeaway

Choose size, compare energy, read stars

Three practical reading steps

Connect the findings to comparing similar-sized individual models. Link official label guidance.

Reader leaves with: A comparison method, rather than a universal winning technology.

06 / Transparency

Source, processing and limits

Source CSV + README linksAI declaration + snapshot boundary

Document filtering, rounding, means, privacy, ethics and AI assistance. Keep February Lab 3 separate from October Demo 1.

Reader can check: How the story was produced and what it cannot establish.

On mobile, panels follow the same order in one column. Charts have labelled values and data-table alternatives; the story does not require hover or colour discrimination.
